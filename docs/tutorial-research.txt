TANGER — FIRST TUTORIAL RESEARCH AND SCOPE
October 10, 2026

The user requested an intentionally dry tutorial, not a finished game made from
one prompt. Current scope: start/loading/settings screens, one authored training
room, one original operator, one training unit, attacks, dashes, and impact pacing.

REFERENCE RESEARCH

The ZZZ developer interview discusses approachable controls, frame-sensitive
dodge interruption, and the contribution of visual/audio feedback to responsive
combat. The same interview describes reworking early content; it is a December
2024 source, not a verified walkthrough of the current live tutorial. [1]

The producer's launch overview describes character switching, assists, and
chained attacks. Those are reference context, not features implemented here. [2]

I searched for current introductory tutorial footage and found public prologue
walkthroughs, but the video fetch was throttled. No claim is made that a current
video was watched or that TANGER reproduces ZZZ's exact lesson sequence. The
prototype's drill order is an original engineering interpretation: movement,
basic contact, dash, timed evasion, then combined practice. [3]

IMPLEMENTATION CHOICES

- Keep instructions literal and short. Advance when the action is completed.
  No dialogue, cinematic introduction, plot, missions, or rewards.
- A successful strike briefly freezes combat motion, then slows recovery before
  normal speed returns. Input, pause, and dash cooldown use real time.
- A dash can cancel an attack. The training unit uses a visible windup before its
  attack; dashing during contact avoids damage. Missed strikes do not cause hit stop.
- The unit cannot be permanently killed. It records contacts and continues drills.
  The player recovers at spawn on defeat; this is a practice room, not a death system.
- Settings adjust quality, camera sensitivity, camera shake, effects volume, and
  impact slowdown. Reduced-motion preferences default camera shake to off.
- All gameplay/settings state is in memory. Community-hosted servers are the
  intended future direction; no server browser, networking, accounts, cloud saves,
  centralized progression authority, or anti-cheat is implemented in this build.

LIMITS TO REVIEW NEXT

The operator uses an original articulated mesh hierarchy with procedural joint
animation. It is not a production motion-captured or fully skinned character.
Movement is grounded and uses circle-versus-box collision on the authored floor;
there is no jumping, climbing, gliding, general rigid-body physics, or navmesh.
The camera stays within room bounds rather than using full geometry shape casts.
Timing values and visual polish are provisional and meant for feedback.
